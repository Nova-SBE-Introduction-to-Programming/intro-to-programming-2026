Why this course is different

You'll work the way a developer joins a company.

First

Read

You get a real codebase on day one. You ask it questions, you find your way around, you change one thing and see it work.

Then

Build

You add features, first to our app, then to your own. AI writes most of the code. You decide what to build and whether it's right.

Finally

Ship

Something deployed, with a URL, that other people can click. And you can explain every part of it.

The one rule

AI is allowed everywhere.
You can't ship what you can't explain.

Lists, loops, functions, classes — you'll meet all of them. Not to memorise them, but to read what the AI wrote, catch it when it's wrong, and tell it what you actually want.

Class 1 · The skill nobody teaches

Every app is a workflow,
drawn precisely.

Most people who fail at building software don't fail at code — they fail at describing the process. AI can write the code. It cannot decide your workflow.

Trigger

What starts it? Someone pays. A lead calls. A form arrives.

Steps

What happens, in what order? One box, one action.

Decisions

Where does it branch? Every branch needs an answer for both sides.

Data

What does each step need to know — and where does that come from?

Land the claim hard: this is the course's core skill. Walk one everyday example on the board first (ordering pizza: trigger → choose → pay → track → deliver, and the data each step needs) so the exercise prompt isn't the first workflow they ever see.
Class 1 · Exercise · 20 minutes

Design the workflow.

Four friends go on a weekend trip. They pay for things at different times, in different amounts — and some things are only for some of them. Design the workflow that ends with everyone square.

Do

Groups of 3–4, paper or whiteboard. Boxes for steps, diamonds for decisions, arrows for order. For every step, write the information it needs — and where that information comes from.

Rules

No code, no app names. One clear trigger, one clear end state ("everyone is square"). Every decision has both branches drawn. If a step needs data nobody wrote down earlier, your workflow has a hole.

Walk the room. Push on the holes: What if the payer is also in the split? Who records the expense, and when? What if two people remember different amounts? Pick 2–3 groups with different shapes to present in the debrief. Common gap: they draw the happy path and skip decisions entirely.
Class 1 · Debrief

What you drew is the software.

Your nouns

→ data

Trip, person, expense, share. The things your steps talk about become the tables — db.py.

Your steps

→ screens

"Add an expense", "see who owes whom". Where a person acts, the app needs a screen — app.py.

Your decisions

→ logic

Who's in this split? How much each? The branches and calculations become the rules — logic.py.

A bad workflow makes a bad app, no matter how good the AI. A precise one is already half the prompt. Next class, you'll open an app that was built from exactly this workflow.

Have 2–3 groups show their drawing, 1 min each. Compare shapes: where did they branch, what data did they name. Don't open the app — the paper-to-running-app reveal belongs to Class 2. End on the teaser.
